# Plantation Road cut infill

The existing terrain clip for road index 36109 had four overlapping OSM-tunnel opening rectangles. `build-barker-cut-fill.py` triangulates their union into a local infill below the generated road surface; the scene also adds retaining faces around the clipped boundary, skipping sampled road mouths. It does not alter road ribbons, AI paths or the tram alignment. A separate visual view includes the existing road ribbon and terrain cuts.

The fill covers approximately 534.27 m² of the 534.38 m² union. Tiny triangulation omissions and exposed seams can remain; the boundary faces are deliberately omitted near road junctions, and the two existing bridge-end support pairs are still rudimentary. This does **not** establish accurate stone abutments, complete slopes, pedestrian access, collision for the new retaining faces, or an in-game driving result. Road and rail clearance checks are discrete samples only, and road/track elevations remain unsurveyed.

The opening triangulation now clips and retriangulates boundary cells rather than discarding triangles that cross the union boundary. Its summed plan area is 534.375600 m² against the 534.375601 m² opening union (rounding tolerance); the previous result omitted roughly 0.10 m². Infill triangle winding also faces upward. This fixes missing infill *within that union* only: the isolated bridge preview still shows sky-coloured gaps at road mouths / adjoining terrain, which need a separate junction solution and full-scene validation.

Where the hillside is lower than the clipped road-opening floor, a separate below-road edge fill now joins the floor down to the existing terrain. It does not add an above-road wall across a junction: retaining faces continue to be omitted near road mouths. The isolated abutment preview now also draws the three adjoining road ribbons, making the junction context less misleading. Samples of the added triangles, including edge midpoints and centroids, clear the road and the nominal tram deck; this is not a continuous vehicle-sweep or driving test.

The opposite case remains unresolved: at both ends, portions of the DTM are higher than the road at the opening boundary. Filling those gaps vertically would obstruct traffic, while leaving them open can expose sky from some views. They need a wider, road-aware junction regrade and full-world inspection; the new below-grade fill does not resolve that problem. The simple local road preview is not the production road mesh and is not a road continuity validation.

A junction-apron experiment was rejected: samples found its interpolated surface up to 0.36 m above an adjoining road, and its outer edge did not consistently meet the original terrain. It was not integrated. The investigation also found two edge samples of the existing infill about 9 mm above road level despite its earlier centroid-only check. The provisional infill datum is now 0.25 m below its sampled road reference instead of 0.12 m; checking vertices, edge midpoints and centroids yields a minimum sampled road-to-infill clearance of about 0.121 m. This does not guarantee continuous clearance or resolve the sky gaps; an exact multi-road junction envelope and matching boundary are still needed before attempting a wider regrade.
