# Passing-loop terrain correction

The previous trial profile ran about 6–7 m below the DTM through much of the lower passing loop. A narrow clipping strip and vertical retaining faces made this look like a deep trench. That was an artefact of fitting an unsurveyed rail profile to a coarse terrain model, not evidence of the real tramway. Reducing the solver's smoothness penalty did not materially improve the 475–700 m difference because the provisional May Road overbridge constraint and maximum gradient restrict the profile; the experiment was not adopted.

As an interim local scene correction, the DTM mesh from chainage 515–730 m is replaced in a maximum 32 m corridor by 1 m sampled grading that meets the existing rail bed and blends sideways back to the DTM. The first 15 m transition expands from the old narrow cut so the pre-existing approach remains connected. Existing original-height loop retaining faces now follow the adjusted surface rather than hiding the track in a deep slot. Ground queries use the same grading function before road selection; roads keep priority. Tests sample both branch centres, road footprints and raycast mesh/height consistency; the isolated WebGL preview is `tests/visual/peak-tram-loop-grading.html`.

This is a **provisional terrain adjustment**, not a surveyed slope or a claim that all 1.2 km has been corrected. It lowers ground by up to approximately 7 m over a wide area to fit the current trial profile, so landscape, tree placement, nearby buildings and the ends need inspection in the complete game. Lower stretches before s=515 still have deep cuts, particularly near road-over-rail crossings, and require a combined road/track/terrain redesign rather than an unverified global height shift. Queries approximate the 1 m triangular mesh; they are not exact collision geometry. Track mechanics and operation are not implemented.

## Further discrete hillside work

The provisional grading now also covers s=155–216, 310–323, 346–358 and 395–460 m, and the loop section starts at s=484 m to include May Road's platform. The first 15 m of each section widens from the existing narrow cut, with a 4.1 m inner width at May Road for the platform. The last 15 m narrows again. These ranges were chosen to avoid sampled road surfaces, not as proof of surveyed earthworks. The grade triangulation contains 28,002 triangles in about 12,686 m²; triangle winding was corrected so upward normals are not inverted in WebGL.

A discrete road-envelope test samples 0.5 m laterally, with 0.2 m extra road margin, plus mesh/height raycasts. The isolated `tests/visual/peak-tram-hillside-grading.html` preview shows the s≈425 m area; small sky-coloured cracks remain at a transition into the existing cut. The full game, continuous road clearances, surrounding buildings and vegetation, May Road platform accessibility, and all joins have **not** been verified. Road crossings and the other deep cuts remain unresolved; these local adjustments do not establish that the complete line is at the correct height.
