# Victoria Park rebuild — stage 1: bandstand

## Research and scope
Several rounds of image and official-source searches were made before modelling. See `references/hongkong/victoria-park/README.md` for the saved attributed images and further sources. A 2009 photo by Chong Fat shows that the existing octagonal, dark-roofed gazebo was inappropriate: this is an open stage with a light twin-peaked tensile canopy, grey stone rear wall, slim posts, cables and spotlights. The current interpretation replaces that gazebo within the mapped OSM roof outline (way 149401985, © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL), not a surveyed site plan. Dimensions, orientation, and exact current appearance are inferred; more recent ground-level photographs should be checked.

The pavilion now has a curved fabric canopy with smooth normals and seams, masonry joints, stage, posts, cables and night-switched emissive fixtures plus two local point lights. It retains the existing terrain-derived terrace and uses local colliders for the wall and posts; no wall is placed across the front. Roof vertices have been checked against the OSM outline and sampled road envelopes; the park's existing road-avoidance tests also pass. Neither these checks nor the isolated WebGL day/night views constitute full-world driving, walking, engineering-height, or WebGPU verification.

This is only the bandstand, **not a completed Victoria Park rebuild**. Sports ground materials and orientation, pool building, model boat pool, central lawn, circulation, entrances, broader night lighting and terrain/road interfaces still need detailed photo comparison and testing. The background in the isolated pavilion preview deliberately lacks the full terrain and should not be read as proof of in-world ground continuity.

### Model boat pool: location correction, not a completed rebuild

The previous illustrative centre `(2890, -135)` was about 105 m from an independently researched POI at 22.282319, 114.187098. A second [Family Action listing](https://www.familyaction.hk/activity/victoria-park-model-boat-pool-hong-kong-island) gives directions to 22.28222, 114.187024, roughly 13 m away from that POI. Both are approximate points, not measured pool boundaries. The existing 954 m² inferred outline is now centred on the former point; its rotation and shape remain unverified. The [government dataset](https://data.gov.hk/en-data/dataset/hk-lcsd-facility-facility-mbp/resource/da6fbc8a-f311-4959-9539-15f9f5b20b3d/view/5b6b8aa9-953a-4339-a072-52cea2c5c70f) confirms area and depth but its listed coordinate is for Victoria Park, not independently a surveyed pool centre. The [2021 refurbishment](https://uccl.hk/en/j308/) means old photographs may not reflect the current surface or edge details. Check its project photographs and obtain properly licensed recent references before redesigning the pool. The inferred location has only been checked against sampled roads, facilities and generated path centrelines; no complete in-game terrain, night lighting or driving validation has been made.

The pool now uses a restrained blue-green procedural surface and two small illustrative model boats, with two bounded night lights at existing generated path lanterns rather than new posts in roads or water. Isolated WebGL day/night views are available in `tests/visual/victoria-model-boat-pool.html`; these omit the full world's ground and tree layer. Recent online photos show a curved dividing promontory, tile and surrounding trees, a provisional rounded dividing promontory now interrupts the inferred water outline, but its exact orientation and dimensions remain unresolved: this remains a photo-directed approximation, **not a finished reconstruction**. The 2021 works and undocumented exact outline limit certainty. Neither complete WebGPU rendering, full terrain transitions nor actual driving has been validated.

The provisional divider is dry paved geometry, excluded from the water polygon and tree/furniture placement, with a matching ground-height query. The inferred remaining water polygon is scaled to 954 m²; LCSD describes that as the facility area, not a detailed surveyed water boundary. Sampling checks road and park containment but do not prove continuous clearance, a smooth connection to surrounding DTM, or walkability; those require full-world validation. Its side, dimensions and overall pool perimeter still need a current measured plan or georeferenced aerial.

An 8 m provisional graded connection has been added at the divider's landward end. The visible mesh and ground query share the same DTM-based function, tapering laterally and returning to the park base height at the outer edge. On a 0.2 m sampled grid the largest lift above park base is approximately 0.394 m; sampled longitudinal grade reaches approximately 7.3%, but this is **not** an accessibility certification. Road and other facility exclusions were checked at 0.25 m samples, and rendered vertices agree with the query to 1 mm apart from the intentional 8 mm draw offset. This does not establish continuous clearance, actual walking, full-world terrain continuity, or an as-built design.

### Queen Victoria monument: approximate site and rendering investigation

The earlier scene placed the monument near `(2845, 65)`. [Wikipedia's monument entry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statue_of_Queen_Victoria,_Hong_Kong) gives 22.281115, 114.189348, projecting to approximately `(3065.734, 86.926)`, near Causeway Road and the football pitches. It is a mapped point, not a survey or a verified pedestal outline; the model has been moved there and the generated approach re-routed. Sampled park/road/feature and path checks pass, not a full in-game clearance test. The existing statue model remains a crude placeholder; an exploratory procedural replacement looked unlike the photo and was discarded rather than represented as complete. The saved Minghong photo in the reference folder shows a much more detailed seated figure with a carved high-backed throne, layered robes and dark bronze finish. More detailed sculpture and site lighting remain to be done.

During the discarded sculpture trial, a night WebGL view showed playing surfaces turning yellow-white. The committed baseline did not reproduce this in the same view after the prototype was removed. The park's custom node-based tint and emissive materials now explicitly use `MeshStandardNodeMaterial` rather than attaching nodes to `MeshStandardMaterial`; this avoids relying on automatic conversion when shader variants are introduced. `tests/visual/victoria-queen.html?night` provides a scene-order stress view with samples, and the separate court view supports `?night`. Both day and night court samples were checked locally in isolated WebGL; this is not proof of full-world WebGPU or lighting correctness.

#### Pedestal study, not the finished sculpture

A separate [2019 park description](https://hong-kong-travelblog.com/nature-outdoors/victoria-park-the-biggest-park-on-hong-kong-island/) and [Chinese Victoria Park entry](https://zh.wikipedia.org/zh-hk/%E7%B6%AD%E5%A4%9A%E5%88%A9%E4%BA%9E%E5%85%AC%E5%9C%92) both describe the stone pedestal as seven feet high (approximately 2.13 m; source independence and measurement method are unknown). The former's LCSD-attributed image shows a tall near-rectangular pale stone pedestal with a moulded stepped top, not large ground-level steps. The retained Minghong 2008 CC BY-SA photograph shows the moulded upper courses and joints in more detail. The old five-metre-wide stack has been replaced with a 2.12 m high, approximately 3.13 × 2.88 m estimate, subdivided by restrained joints; its width, exact stone courses, forecourt and bearing remain unverified. The provisional dark bronze placeholder remains visibly crude and is **not a finished representation of the statue**. Dedicated monument lighting remains undone. The pedestal and the new darker bronze material were checked in isolated WebGL day/night scenes; the night statue remains too dark, and the isolated view omits the full terrain and trees. Full WebGPU/in-game visual, walking, lighting and road conflicts remain unverified.
