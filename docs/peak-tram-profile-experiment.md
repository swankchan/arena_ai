# Peak Tram vertical-profile feasibility experiment

Run `node tools/cities/hongkong/check-peak-tram-profile.mjs`. This is a diagnostic, not an active railway, a surveyed alignment, a smooth ride profile or a clearance certification. It samples each OSM branch at up to 2 m spacing; constrains known road-over and tram-over intersections using the nearest returned road surface and a **provisional** 6.3 m vertical envelope; fixes the final approach to the existing upper station stub's estimated rail level; and looks for a non-descending profile with grade at most tan(26°). It works backwards to determine feasible height intervals, then greedily chooses heights near the scene DTM. An unknown road crossing is not constrained. Road dataset IDs are local indices, not OSM way IDs.

Current result: the independent branches each have a mathematical solution under these simplified sampled constraints. Both start at roughly 41.04 m and meet the upper stub at 395.45 m. The solutions deviate by up to about 7.92 m above and 6.40 m below the DTM, and can change grade by about 0.488 between adjacent samples. This is **not a usable rail design**: these sharp grade changes need vertical curves, and the 41.04 m lower terminus is an artefact of the assumed envelope and road elevation, not a measured station level. We have not verified that independently solved branches meet at identical heights at both ends of the passing loop, or that the upper stub itself is correct.

A road query returns only one nearest surface, and centreline sample spacing does not ensure continuous or swept-car clearance, lateral clearance, bridge structural depth, road approaches, other road levels, track support, terrain cutting, stations or accessible routes. The unknown pedestrian crossing still needs classification. No terrain, road, bridge or runtime scene is modified. Before building, obtain better station and crossing evidence, jointly fit both loop branches with continuous grade transitions, account for car and bridge dimensions, and check actual road, terrain, colliders and traffic in-game. A successful result here only means that these simplified inequalities can be satisfied.
