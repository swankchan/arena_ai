# Central Terminus: inferred concourse and moving-walkway connection

[Arup's project account](https://www.arup.com/en-us/projects/peak-tram-upgrade-project/) describes approximately 40 m of inclined moving walkways in a glazed, aluminium-clad spiral portal, a larger lower-ground waiting area, and a steep-gradient platform. The photographs inform the muted grey upper panels, warm timber-like lower panels, glass balustrades and linear lighting. The **plan, elevations, roof shape and access arrangements are not surveyed** and are scene-fit estimates, not a reconstruction of the real plan.

The existing street bay and trial platform are about 99 m apart in the scene. This model uses a 2.8 m simulated lift descent at the bay, a narrow lower hall and a 40 m rising walkway section. The hallway narrows near the platform to fit the existing platform, so it is **not a representation of the real 1,000+ person hall**. The line ends at a newly opened portal in the platform's end wall. It has warm day/night strips and local point lights. It is deliberately under the modeled roads, which are not moved. A local terrain opening is provided around the entrance shaft; the early passage ceiling is fitted below the terrain instead of cutting a long open trench. This is an approximation, not verified underground construction.

The inferred lift automatically calls/returns and carries a person who waits inside. Two estimated opposing travelator lanes move a person standing on them; an animated tread texture follows the same simulated directions. The mechanism and handrails are simplified. The lift location is inferred to reconcile the model with existing road levels and does not claim the real lift is at this location. The upper and lower safety covers and automatic motion are gameplay simplifications, not actual doors or an accessibility-compliant installation. Person-mode ground queries select the lower floor only when the reference height is close; vehicle road selection remains unchanged. Train-door alignment, glazed screen gates, station-scale waiting capacity and skylights are not complete; the platform now has an inferred cover, not a verified bridge deck.

Tests sample the trial floor, road surface, roof/side-wall geometry and wall collision boxes, test a local centreline against the station entrance/tower/lift, raycast the station-end portal and shaft opening, and exercise lift and walkway kinematics. With a 0.35 m road query buffer and 0.4 m samples, the selected floor had over 4.25 m to sampled road height; this is not the underside of a measured deck, a continuous collision proof, a structural survey or accessibility certification. `tests/visual/peak-tram-central-concourse.html` is an isolated WebGL preview, including `?night` and `?portal`; complete-game WebGPU, player traversal, lift interaction, road traffic, adjacent buildings and all terrain seams remain unverified. The cut/junction gaps and full-scene platform-cover fit still need work.

The inferred lower hall also includes metal queue rails and four illuminated, original schematic heritage panels. The reference folder contains a credited Wikimedia Commons thumbnail of a real 2022 history screen; the in-game panels are original illustrations, not the real display videos. Queue arrangement and screen locations are scene-fit estimates.

`tests/visual/peak-tram-central-controller-check.html` runs the actual person controller against a **local test world** using the inferred station, St John's, local colliders and road-height selection. A scripted round trip rode the lift down, walked the centre path, turned onto the platform, returned and rode up; maximum sampled per-frame vertical change on the outward walk was about 0.15 m. This is not an end-to-end full-world traversal or a wheelchair simulation. A separate full-world software-WebGL attempt did not finish initializing within 320 seconds (road-data processing was at 48%); it therefore supplies no full-game validation.

A later sandbox full-world software-WebGL attempt progressed further: the unified world, road markings, traffic and harbour setup reported ready, then shader precompilation reached 145/1,954 jobs. A watchdog stopped the browser when host available memory fell below 900 MB (the sandbox has approximately 3.9 GB total). This is not proof of an application error or a completed first frame, and the station, roads and accessibility remain unverified in the complete game. Loading-status steps now distinguish traffic-aid completion from later world and traffic setup; the older apparent stop at the traffic-aids label did not identify the actual stalled stage.

The portal end was regraded to meet the trial platform centre and eased across its angled width. A scripted person-controller round trip and local floor raycasts passed; the joint has not been verified across the complete terrain cut or with a wheelchair. The platform's new steep crossfall and longitudinal slope are documented in `peak-tram-central-platform.md` and remain unresolved.

A small triangular floor apron now closes the positive-offset wedge where the oblique corridor ended short of the platform's end line. It has a matching person-height query and is locally raycast-tested against the platform edge and sampled road heights. This closes that particular plan-view wedge, not every terrain, ceiling or structural seam in the full world.
