# Brewin Path to May Road

The active trial track continues approximately 107 m from OSM node 26412146 to the May Road stop node, including the rail bed, sleepers, retaining faces and local terrain cuts. A scene-fit stone barrel vault sits under the fully road-covered core of the May Road crossing (road ribbons 36167/42840); road surfaces and vehicle paths are not changed. Historical sources describe May Road bridge and a west-side platform above it, including [Gwulo](https://gwulo.com/media/23602) and [Checkerboard Hill](https://www.checkerboardhill.com/2011/02/the-peak-tram-trainspotters-edition/). The vault is an estimate based on the scene's trial profile, not surveyed dimensions or a faithful finished bridge: only its central 6 m is modelled; skew, road approaches, full facade and parapet remain unfinished. A provisional western stepped platform, shelter and bilingual information board are installed near the May Road stop node. Its shared platform design is not a confirmed reproduction of the real station; the 2021 [station sign photo](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:May_Road_stop_sign_20210220_152449.jpg) informed the brown-and-green information board, which is simplified and non-interactive. The platform is roughly 20 m, shorter than the 33 m car. Access stairs, request buttons, boarding, and accurate surroundings remain unfinished. The sign's published 180 m elevation is not the height of the stop node in the trial profile (about 192 m); this discrepancy is unresolved.

The generic profile approach test samples road overlap, retaining-wall tops, supports and nearby street lights. The May Road vault test samples the arch relative to the queried road and a hypothetical car envelope. None is continuous swept-volume, construction, actual road geometry, vegetation or building clearance proof. `tests/visual/peak-tram-may-road-bridge.html` is an isolated forced-WebGL view without roads and shows terrain-cut edge gaps; it is not a full game check. General ground queries still use the original DTM; track is not walkable or a working railway. The line continues another approximately 40 m above the stop to the lower loop switch node, with estimated cutting and retaining faces. Terrain samples there place the track up to about 7.7 m below the original ground. The lower portion of the two-branch passing loop is now installed as a partial trial (`docs/peak-tram-passing-loop.md`); its upper connection and track to the upper terminus are not yet installed; `tests/visual/peak-tram-may-road-station.html` shows the station only in isolation.
