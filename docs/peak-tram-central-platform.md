# Provisional Central Terminus platform and approach

The 2022 Central Terminus boarding point was moved uphill from the entrance beneath St John's Building. The OSM stop `n2525250101` and first bridge node `n2525250108` span s=0–32.69 m on the existing **unsurveyed** route/profile. The model uses that span for a pair of side platforms, rails, sleepers, yellow edges, side retaining faces and night wall-light strips, with a local terrain cut. Rail top is 0.28 m above the bed; platform tops are 0.57 m above the rail. The next static track section continues to the existing Kennedy Road bridge; some estimated piers support the elevated parts and narrow cuts clear the buried parts. Platform length, widths, heights, wall and support design are scene-fit estimates, **not measured construction plans**. The calculated profile rises about 10.4 m over the platform; neither this gradient nor level boarding with the 33 m tram has been verified.

The Cotton Tree Drive road ribbon is left untouched above the cutting. At 0.5 m along-track and 0.25 m lateral intervals, road query plus 0.25 m buffer is at least 5.2 m above the rail within the estimated tram width; this is not a continuous swept-volume test or a structural bridge check. The platform ground query is restricted to person mode and near the trial platform level so it does not replace overhead vehicle road heights. Support-box road checks are also discrete. The actual vehicle, platform/train doors, verified road deck/abutments and passenger simulation are not yet implemented. An inferred platform cover was subsequently added; see below. An inferred lift and passage were subsequently added; see below. The two rendered rail sections join geometrically; the full train ride remains unverified.

`tests/visual/peak-tram-central-platform.html` is an isolated WebGL preview containing only simplified terrain, road 720, platform and approach. Small sky-coloured gaps remain at cutting junctions, particularly near the station end and the road edge. This preview is not a full scene or WebGPU check; dynamic road traffic, adjacent buildings, supports, terrain joins, trees, station accessibility, night lighting and overhead clearances must be checked before calling the station complete.

The person-mode floor query now selects the provisional platform below an overhead road even where that road lacks a layer flag, when the reference height is closer to the platform. Vehicle mode retains the original road-selection rules. This is a local height-query correction, not a tested pedestrian route or proof of accessibility.

## Street connection: geometry diagnostic, not an approved route

`node tools/cities/hongkong/check-peak-tram-central-passage.mjs` samples a **hypothetical straight** 4 m wide passage from the current entrance bay to the side of the lower platform. The estimated horizontal length is 97.12 m and rise 4.67 m. With no lift, its slope is 4.81%, but sampled road 35962 is only 1.37 m above the hypothetical floor at the worst point. Even lowering the entrance end by 2 m (requiring a lift not yet designed) gives a 6.87% grade and only 2.94 m to road surface, with 166 sampled points below a 3.4 m road-to-floor allowance. Road surface is not the underside of the deck; these numbers **do not establish headroom**. The 2 m-lift concept is therefore not sufficient as a finished solution. The diagnostic makes no allowance for landings, structural thickness, neighbouring building foundations, existing underground spaces, wall openings or lift accessibility. This straight-line diagnostic is not a safety certification; a different scene-fit lift-and-passage concept was subsequently added as described below.

The provisional floor now has estimated grey tile joints and a tactile-style strip based on the engineer's platform photograph; the prior yellow edge remains for gameplay visibility. The strip is a visual approximation, not an accessibility assessment. The real automatic platform gates and docking equipment are still absent; tram-door alignment and safe boarding remain unverified.

A new inferred concourse approaches a portal cut in the lower end wall. The estimated entrance lift and walkway remain subject to full-scene and accessibility testing; see `peak-tram-central-concourse.md`.

## Scene-fit platform cover

A segmented, gently pitched roof/lining, side glazing and warm night lights now cover the provisional platform. Its height adapts beneath the sampled Cotton Tree Drive road surface and keeps a nominal 3.85 m clearance above the track at sampled mesh locations. This is an inferred visual fit, **not** the bridge structure or verified swept-volume clearance of a 33 m moving tram. The road ribbon and traffic rules are unchanged. Tests check roof, ribs, glazing and lamps against sampled road surfaces and a nominal train envelope; the upper track end remains open, and the terrain/portal joins, full scene, adjacent buildings, traffic and night lighting still require in-game verification. This does not provide automatic platform screen gates, train-door alignment, or prove barrier-free boarding.

The inferred platform edge now has fixed translucent screen panels and four 1.7 m closed placeholder gate bays per side, arranged from the **visual** car’s nominal four doors. The panels and placeholder gates have colliders, so the track is not accessible through them. This is not an operational automatic platform screen-door system: there is no train, interlock, door motion, verified car-door alignment or accessible boarding. Position and dimensions are provisional. An isolated WebGL view and sampled road-clearance checks do not validate the full scene; an overhead infill reduces the uphill end gap, while the track portal remains open to the sky in the isolated preview.

The placeholder gate bays use a trial rigid 33 m visual car centred by 3D distance on the current platform profile. Its measured trial path is about 34.32 m in 3D (32.69 m in plan); the tilted car's door centres at chest height do not fall at even eighths of the plan length. The gate bays now follow those projected centres. The previous evenly-spaced uphill bay was approximately 0.98 m off that trial centre. This is only a geometrical study: the car is not installed in the game, its model doors are not functional, and door widths, vertical gaps, end clearances, curves and the real station position remain unverified.

`node tools/cities/hongkong/check-peak-tram-central-docking.mjs` quantifies why this cannot yet be presented as accessible boarding: the original trial platform was approximately 0.23–0.52 m **below** the visual car floor at the four doors. The platform edge has since been regraded to meet that nominal floor plane; a plan gap of about 0.30 m remains to the car side (0.37 m to the screen line). These are calculations for a rigid, tilted placeholder car against the scene-fit profile, not measurements of the real station. A sampled visual car roof has roughly 0.46 m to the fitted roof in this trial; that is not a continuous moving-vehicle or structural clearance check. `tests/visual/peak-tram-central-platform.html?detail&night&car` shows this isolated trial car but does not install it in the playable world.

A further **unapplied** trial projects the visual car floor across both platforms. In 0.25 m longitudinal samples at six offsets, it would leave only about 2.22 m to the existing roof and 3.04 m to road surface at the worst samples. Simply raising the whole platform to the car is therefore not a sufficient solution; roof/road structure and the concourse transition must be redesigned together. Road surface is not the underside of a measured bridge.

## Trial platform regrade and portal transition

The two platform edges now follow the tilted trial car floor plane, blending back across the width to the previous outer-edge height. Screen panels, tactile details, ground queries and the corridor portal have been adjusted with the floor. The last six metres of the passage ease towards the oblique platform join; local raycasts checked mesh/query agreement and a scripted person-controller round trip still passed. At sampled locations the roof-to-floor minimum was approximately 2.42 m and road-surface-to-floor minimum 3.23 m. These samples are not proof of continuous clearance, nor is road surface a bridge underside.

This is **not an accessibility solution**: the estimated blended crossfall reaches approximately 11% (reduced from approximately 16%) and the longitudinal slope reaches approximately 34%; there is no wheelchair simulation, operating gate, bridge plate or functional train. These steep grades and the remaining gap require a coordinated redesign, not certification or a claim of step-free boarding. The centre path was tested only in a local scene, not the complete game.

The outer platform edge is raised where the provisional roof allows, with a long, smooth transition to a small recess at the constrained uphill crossing. The edge was resampled every 0.25 m so rendered floor triangles follow the ground query; sleepers and tactile marks retain their original approximate spacing. Sampled maximum crossfall is about 11%, down from 16%, while sampled maximum longitudinal grade remains around 34%. A raycast against the actual roof mesh at selected floor positions found at least about 2.50 m clearance. Both slopes are still too steep to represent an accessible route; these are game-geometry checks, not a surveyed road-deck clearance or a wheelchair validation.

A proposed platform-edge extension was **not applied**: the trial visual car has window/door-frame protrusions out to about 1.48 m from the track centre, not merely the nominal 1.35 m body side. The current 1.65 m edge therefore leaves roughly 0.17 m to those static protrusions; moving it to 1.52 m would leave only 0.04 m. Neither value accounts for motion, curves, suspension or real dimensions, so the gap cannot responsibly be closed until a moving swept-envelope and a suitable operational bridge plate are designed.
