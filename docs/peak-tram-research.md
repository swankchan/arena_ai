# Victoria Peak, Peak Tram and the Peak Circle Walk: research in progress

This is research, not a claim that the railway, buildings or walk have been built or verified in-game.

## Railway and operation

The [operator's 2022 sixth-generation announcement](https://www.thepeak.com.hk/sites/peak/files/2022-03/Peak%20Tram_6th%20Generation%20Tramcar%20Unveiling_Press%20Releaes_Eng_Final.pdf) says the current Swiss-built car is bespoke Peak Tram Green, has panoramic windows and 210-passenger capacity. It describes a double reversible funicular with a roughly 1.4 km route, gradients 6–26 degrees, an upper terminus within the Peak Tower, and gives elevations 48 m and 396 m. Its [2022 launch announcement](https://www.thepeak.com.hk/sites/peak/files/2022-08/Announce%20Launch%20Date%20of%206th%20generation%20Peak%20Tram_ENG_FINAL.pdf) mentions accessible level platforms and the refurbished covered Central Terminus. A photo of the sixth-generation car shows a dark green body, light roof, large windows and front headlights; the red fifth-generation car is not the current one. Other operator publications give the lower terminus as 28 m, so these figures must not be used as a surveyed rail profile without reconciliation.

The [government film-location page](https://fpf.ccidahk.gov.hk/en/location/detail.php?id=191) describes four intermediate stations. These are Kennedy Road, MacDonnell Road, May Road and Barker Road; do not mistake the former Bowen Road stop for a current station. The [HK Shopper route description](https://thehkshopper.com/98034-the-new-peak-tram-attractions-at-victoria-peak.html) describes two cars, a passing loop and curves. Exact current operations and stops still need verification before implementing interactive riding.

OSM track geometry can be traced through [way 265943515](https://api.openstreetmap.org/api/0.6/way/265943515/full.json) (May Road and MacDonnell Road), passing-loop ways [265943512](https://api.openstreetmap.org/api/0.6/way/265943512/full.json) and [265943513](https://api.openstreetmap.org/api/0.6/way/265943513/full.json), and [85404052](https://api.openstreetmap.org/api/0.6/way/85404052/full.json) (bridge). [Upper station node 26412045](https://api.openstreetmap.org/api/0.6/node/26412045/ways.json) is on way 1117280223, marked as a building passage. These are only part of the route: identify and connect the remaining ways before constructing it. OSM gauge tags on the fetched ways are 1524 mm. OSM data © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL. OSM geometry and DTM are not surveyed railway engineering profiles; bridges and road crossings require separate clearance and collision checks.

## Buildings and walk

[Hong Kong Tourism Board](https://www.discoverhongkong.com/eng/place-to-go/travel.guide-peak-circle-walk.html) describes the distinctive wok-shaped Peak Tower above the tram station and the separate Peak Galleria with a third-floor observation deck. The two buildings face one another, not a single building. [Operator nature-walk information](https://www.thepeak.com.hk/en/the-peak-experience/nature-walks) describes a loop from Peak Tower via Lugard Road and Harlech Road, with a bridge section on the hillside. HKTB gives the circuit as about 3.5 km. The existing `tools/data/hongkong/roads_minor_sw.json` has OSM geometry for both roads, including bridge-tagged Lugard Road sections. Whether those segments already render and are walkable, and how their elevations and bridge clearance compare with DTM, remains to be measured.

Reference aerial photograph: [Zolima CityMag Peak Tower](https://zolimacitymag.com/hong-kong-skyline-icons-peak-tower-which-one/) shows the long curved white upper shell and roof terrace, with Peak Galleria across the road. Actual dimensions, footprint, entrances, interiors, structural details and playable approaches are not yet established. Do not substitute a rough placeholder for a finished model.

## Circle Walk extraction and terrain check

`tools/cities/hongkong/build-peak-walk.mjs` reproducibly extracts the 14 existing OSM Lugard/Harlech circuit centerlines into `peak-walk-data.js`. The westward Harlech Road branch (way 155738201) is excluded. The extracted chain is approximately 3,482 m in the game projection; its two ends near the Peak plaza are about 7 m apart, not a completed OSM connection. The route is data only: no new surface or connection has been installed in-game.

`node tools/cities/hongkong/check-peak-walk.mjs` samples the current game's DTM at intervals up to 3 m along those ways: roughly 381.4–429.1 m, maximum local grade about 78%, and 711 of 1,570 segments exceed 15%; 60 sample points intersect the existing road query with a 2.25 m margin. These are DTM interpolation diagnostics, **not** measurements of actual walking-path grades or proof of road obstruction. Laying a ribbon directly on this DTM would create an implausibly steep and uneven route, particularly at OSM bridge sections. A walkable implementation needs a defensible path elevation profile and coordinated terrain/bridge treatment, junctions, and an actual-geometry road/traffic check. Don't silently flatten the mountain or claim the raw DEM profile is a surveyed walkway.

## Route tracing and feasibility (2026-10-24)

The data-only `peak-tram-route.js` traces both branches of [OSM relation 3264047](https://www.openstreetmap.org/relation/3264047), © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL. The ways meet at their shared OSM nodes; the upper way extends past the mapped stop inside a `building_passage`. The current mapped Garden Road stop node 2525250101 is distinct from the older station node 279086619 in the relation; the route ends at the current track stop, not at the street entrance. The station area is [way 636324517](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/636324517); the adjacent St John's Building is [way 179091496](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/179091496). The Peak Tower footprint is [way 143716612](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/143716612); the upper terminus is inside it. OSM geometries are mapping, not a surveyed track or construction plan.

`node tools/cities/hongkong/check-peak-tram-route.mjs` projects the trace and samples the current scene's terrain and road query. Each loop branch is about 1,261–1,265 m in horizontal projection, not the official approximately 1.4 km inclined railway length. The terrain samples range about 47.4–396.5 m; 6 of roughly 650 uphill samples descend and up to 18 exceed the official 26-degree maximum. The roads query returns 47 samples per branch, including known bridge crossings; its selection is not evidence of a collision or adequate bridge clearance. The ground model is not the railway's measured vertical alignment, and a terrain-draped track would therefore be misleading. The route's buildings, bridges, platforms, cars and ride interaction have **not** been implemented. Grade-separated road crossings and the relationship between the lower platform and its street entrance need a coherent track-height profile, clearance checks, and visual/play testing before the route can be delivered.
