# 美利大廈 / The Murray

The active Hong Kong scene includes a photo-directed **exterior**, located using [OSM way 85320844 v14](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/85320844), © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL. The mapped oriented footprint is about 29.86 × 51.84 m. [Foster + Partners](https://www.fosterandpartners.com/projects/the-murray) describes the 1969 government building's recessed windows, multi-storey arches and former vehicle ramp, and its conversion to a hotel; their [project data](https://www10.aeccafe.com/blogs/arch-showcase/2018/07/06/the-murray-hotel-in-hong-kong-china-by-foster-partners/) cites 51.5 × 31.5 m and 93.83 m height. Floor-count conventions differ across sources. The simulated construction datum is the maximum mapped footprint-edge terrain sample (about 28.15 m); it is not a surveyed floor elevation. The tower height relative to this datum is approximately 89 m, to account approximately for the lower terrain at the downhill end.

The model has 966 individually recessed, skewed glazed bays with diagonal concrete sunshades, 22 genuinely open arcade arches with curved soffits and individual piers, an inset glazed lobby, bronze trim, a terrain-connected section of the former car ramp, roof edge and terrain-following arcade paving. Bay, arch and entrance dimensions, colours and ramp location are photo-directed approximations, not construction drawings. One narrow ramp section behind the eastern arcade is walkable using the shared height query; its endpoints, width and slope are approximations, and it does not yet form the complete continuous ramp around the building. A small photo-directed lower lobby now has a genuine tree-facing opening, a level dark stone floor, bronze inlay and slatted ceiling, reception and lounge furniture. Its position, dimensions, furnishings and height are approximate; the room is separated from the unmodelled hotel. The terrain mesh is locally cut beneath it, and the pedestrian height query selects its floor near lobby elevation. An approximate terrain-following forecourt and Cassia tree are now included. [OSM node 12718892293 v3](https://www.openstreetmap.org/node/12718892293) maps the Cassia javanica at 22.2779993, 114.1604069 and tags a 17 m height and 1.13 m circumference (© OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL); these are not a current survey. The [Development Bureau](https://www.devb.gov.hk/en/home/my_blog/index_id_279.html) documents the preservation of the tree and podium; the hotel [also mentions a newly planted sapling](https://www.niccolohotels.com/en/the-murray-hong-kong/experiences/the-cassia-javanica-tree), so the present form and condition should be checked. Crown, flowers, paving boundary and circular planter are illustrative rather than measured. Paving follows terrain and excludes road surfaces; the planter has a simple collider. The wider landscaped arrival court, public paths, complete former car-ramp layout and the remainder of the hotel interior are not reproduced; the roof restaurant is an approximation described below. The present model must not be described as a complete hotel or a surveyed replica.

The footprint's DTM edge heights vary roughly 21.9–28.2 m. Piers extend down to the existing terrain and paving samples the same terrain; no global pad or road-height modification was made. The isolated 0.5 m road/vehicle-height collision test checks 20,359 road points with zero hits, and sampled arcade centres have no pier collider. The earlier tower-only isolated preview was rendered with `forceWebGL`; the new courtyard, ramp and lobby have not been visually verified. Full-game WebGPU, walking, driving and the as-built landscaping remain unverified.

Ramp references: [Foster + Partners interview](https://www.architecturaldigest.com/story/foster-partners-murray-hong-kong) and [Architectural Record](https://www.architecturalrecord.com/articles/13326-the-murray-by-foster-partners) describe the former vehicle ramp wrapping the building as a pedestrian walkway and the lobby below it. The simulated single straight section is an approximation and has not been visually or play-tested in the complete scene.

The lobby styling is based on [Foster + Partners interior photographs](https://www.wallpaper.com/travel/foster-partners-the-murray-hotel-hong-kong) and [Architectural Record](https://www.architecturalrecord.com/articles/13326-the-murray-by-foster-partners): dark marble and bronze with an inclined ceiling below the former car ramp. Its entrance and room layout are estimates, not an as-built plan. The tests sample a 0.48 m-radius path through the entrance and confirm no nearby road lies in the terrain cut, but do not substitute for in-game play-testing.

The short-side lobby glazing now meets the long-side wall instead of protruding into the existing ramp section: a 0.48 m-radius pedestrian centreline test covers both endpoints as well as the length. This only repairs the current approximate straight section; it does not complete the wrapped ramp. Foster's published 01-floor plan shows a turn around the core at the tree-side short end; its relationship to the current ground-connected section, lobby levels and terrain remains to be reconciled.

An approximate single automatic lift and guarded roof terrace connect the lobby to the roof as a gameplay feature. Standing at either landing for two seconds recalls the car; standing inside for two seconds sends it to the other landing. The roof has a temporary safety cover while the car is away. This is not a surveyed lift bank. The tests exercise the state machine, walking clearance and the person controller's gravity, but full-game interaction and WebGPU rendering have not been verified. The rest of the hotel interior and the continuous former ramp remain unbuilt.

The rooftop now includes a Popinjays-inspired glass pavilion with bronze frames, dining tables, blue seating, lights and an outdoor terrace. [Foster + Partners' published photographs](https://mooool.com/en/the-murray-by-foster-partner.html) identify the rooftop restaurant; its [opening description](https://www.luxurysocietyasia.com/popinjays/) describes a glazed pavilion, grey/blue/gold interior and 420 m² wraparound terrace. Dimensions, entrances, furniture layout and the single lift remain interpretive, not a reconstruction of the as-built plan. Collision samples cover an accessible route around the lift to the pavilion and both terraces; the full game and the latest reconstruction have not been visually checked.
